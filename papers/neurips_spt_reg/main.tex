\begin{abstract}
We study probabilistic regression under deployment shift, where source-trained
predictive laws become biased, under-dispersed, and poorly calibrated on target
data. We propose Shift-Factored Predictive Transport (SPT-Reg), a test-time
framework that adapts predictive distributions by composing output-space prior
transport, predictive-subspace alignment, and representation-aware uncertainty
inflation. The framework is predictor-family agnostic through a shared
predictive container spanning Gaussian, quantile, histogram, and ordered-bin
regression outputs. Conformal wrapping restores finite-sample coverage, while
prediction-powered inference enables valid target-domain confidence intervals
with limited labels. We show how this factorized transport view improves
calibration, tail coverage, selective prediction, and inferential efficiency
under realistic regression shifts without requiring target retraining.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many regression deployment failures are not caused by a complete collapse of
$p(y \mid x)$, but by target-domain distortions in predictive calibration,
output prevalence, and uncertainty scale. This paper develops the hypothesis
that these failures can often be repaired by transporting the source predictive
law rather than retraining the regressor from scratch.

The branch implementation targets the predictive families that currently carry
the most credible evidence in \texttt{torchregress}: Gaussian, quantile,
bar/histogram, ordered-bin, and sampled predictive laws such as MDNs. The
empirical narrative must separate \emph{finite-sample validity} (coverage,
calibration, conformal and PPI guarantees) from \emph{efficiency} (sharpness,
NLL, CRPS, interval width). At present, the in-repo evidence is often strongest
on validity preservation; claims that transport improves sharpness at fixed
validity should be stated only where the JSON summaries support them.

Our empirical position is deliberately strong: SPT-Reg should be evaluated as a
competing method against target fine-tuning, raw conformalization, feature-only
normalization, and family-matched BinnedPDF baselines, not as an isolated
ablation stack. Benchmark rows now include \emph{transport-only} paths (adapted
laws before conformal widening) and, for MDN, a \emph{conformal-only} baseline
on the source law, so tables do not attribute interval widening solely to
transport when conformal correction dominates.

\section{Method}

SPT-Reg composes five stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item output-space prior transport on a shared support grid,
  \item significance-weighted predictive-subspace alignment,
  \item representation-aware uncertainty inflation,
  \item optional conformal wrapping, and
  \item prediction-powered inference on adapted predictions.
\end{enumerate}

The current branch implementation exposes this flow through a reusable
\texttt{ShiftFactoredPredictiveTransport} orchestrator in
\texttt{torchregress.test\_time}.

Internally, the method maps each predictive family to a common
\texttt{PredictiveBatch} representation and then to a shared support-grid
density. This lets the same transport operator act on Gaussian, quantile,
histogram, ordered-bin / BinnedPDF, and sampled / MDN outputs without changing
the surrounding evaluation contract. In the current implementation, prior
transport is driven by confidence-filtered and locally consistent target rows,
significance-weighted subspace alignment is only applied when the predictor can
be rerun on aligned inputs, and representation-shift calibration inflates
either Gaussian variance or density temperature depending on the predictive
family. Stage~A (prior transport) can be weakened or skipped when target
evidence is weak: configurable floors on the selected-row fraction and caps on
prior divergence (total variation between estimated source and target priors)
reduce shrink weight so transport does not over-react to noisy target histograms.

The branch also treats conformal wrapping and prediction-powered inference as
first-class method stages rather than post-processing afterthoughts. This is
important for the paper narrative: transport is used to improve sharpness and
residual quality under shift, while conformal prediction and PPI restore
finite-sample validity for intervals and downstream estimands.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Dataset shift and covariate shift.}
Training and deployment distributions often differ; the resulting bias is
standardly analyzed as covariate shift, label shift, or broader dataset
shift~\citep{quinonero2009dataset}. Importance-weighting and kernel mean
matching reweight source data to match target feature
marginals~\citep{huang2007correcting}. These methods typically operate on
\emph{inputs} or losses for \emph{retraining}; SPT-Reg instead keeps a fixed
source predictor and transports \emph{output-side} predictive laws using target
unlabeled structure, which is closer in spirit to post-hoc calibration and
correction pipelines than to full domain-adversarial
retraining~\citep{ganin2016domain}.

\paragraph{Conformal and distribution-free uncertainty.}
Split and full conformal prediction yield finite-sample coverage under
exchangeability~\citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}. Under covariate shift, coverage
can fail unless scores or weights are adjusted; weighted and shifted conformal
variants explicitly model tilted test
distributions~\citep{tibshirani2019conformal}. Recent work pushes this line into
\emph{online} and arbitrarily drifting regimes by adaptively tuning conformal
updates rather than assuming a static tilt~\citep{gibbs2024conformal}. SPT-Reg is
complementary: we first transport predictive laws on the target domain, then
apply split conformal as an explicit, auditable stage. \texttt{RawSplitConformalGaussian}
isolates conformal-only widening on the source law; \texttt{SPTTransportGaussian}
isolates transport + inflation \emph{without} conformal, so tables separate
geometric adaptation from validity repair.

\paragraph{Test-time adaptation and target fine-tuning.}
Modern TTA research increasingly documents how adaptation changes not only error
but also the reliability of linear OOD extrapolation
heuristics~\citep{kim2024test}. A strong operational competitor for regression
under shift is still to fine-tune (or refit) on a small labeled target set; our
\texttt{TargetRefitSmallGaussian} row matches that protocol at the calibration
budget. Subspace-aware feature alignment is also an active 2024--2025 theme for
regression TTA (often CV / pose pipelines); our
\texttt{SignificantSubspaceGaussian} row isolates a related \emph{significance-weighted}
alignment path inside the same evaluation harness. Deep ensembles remain a
generic probabilistic baseline~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017deep}; we emphasize
family-matched Gaussian and BinnedPDF rows before claiming dominance over
ensembling in the wild.

\paragraph{Prediction-powered inference.}
PPI uses a small labeled target sample together with unlabeled predictions to
construct valid uncertainty intervals for downstream
estimands~\citep{chernozhukov2023ppi}. In \texttt{torchregress}, PPI metrics are
reported alongside predictive scores when enabled, but the core SPT-Reg narrative
centers on repairing $p(y \mid x)$ under shift before such inferential layers.

\paragraph{Positioning summary.}
Table~\ref{tab:spt_baseline_map} summarizes the large-tabular (Year) Gaussian
comparison rows and their interpretive role; extended rows (BinnedPDF family,
synthetic MDN stress tests) and literature we do \emph{not} yet instantiate as
code baselines are documented in
\texttt{papers/neurips\_spt\_reg/related\_work\_notes.md}.
The conformal and TTA paragraphs above cite post-2023 work~\citep{gibbs2024conformal,kim2024test}
to anchor SPT-Reg relative to current shift and adaptation research, even where
we have not reimplemented every SOTA conformal or TTA variant as a row.

\section{Experiments}

The experimental program is organized into three tracks:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item synthetic factorized-shift benchmarks,
  \item real tabular competing-method evaluations on both a small built-in
  dataset and a larger YearPredictionMSD-style dataset, and
  \item an optional photo-$z$ compatibility track kept lightweight in
  \texttt{torchregress} because the main photo-$z$ effort has moved to
  \texttt{torchz}.
\end{enumerate}

All main tables and figures should be generated from machine-readable summaries
under \texttt{reports/neurips\_spt\_reg/}.

\paragraph{Competing methods.}
The main comparison set includes raw source predictors, feature-stat
normalization, significant-subspace alignment alone, prior transport alone,
raw split conformal, \textbf{covariate-weighted} split conformal (logistic
density-ratio adapter; \citealp{tibshirani2019conformal}), small-label target
refitting, and the full SPT-Reg
pipeline. We report each of these on Gaussian predictors where applicable, and
we additionally compare ordered-bin / BinnedPDF predictors with and without
SPT-Reg in the synthetic and tabular tracks. Sampled MDN predictors are
included as a synthetic multimodal stress test and a competing predictive-law
family, but they are not yet the primary real-data claim path in
\texttt{torchregress}. In \texttt{torchregress}, the photo-$z$ track is treated
as an optional compatibility benchmark rather than the core submission path.

\noindent\textbf{BinnedPDF family.} The same protocol includes

\paragraph{Metrics.}
The primary tables emphasize probabilistic and decision-facing quantities:
negative log-likelihood, CRPS, coverage at $90\%$, interval width,
risk-coverage summaries, and target-domain PPI interval width and coverage.
For the photo-$z$ benchmark we additionally report NMAD, catastrophic outlier
rate, and high-redshift error slices so that improvements in predictive
transport do not hide tail failures that matter in scientific deployment, but
those diagnostics should be considered supporting evidence here unless the
final photo-$z$ evaluation is pulled in from \texttt{torchz}.

\paragraph{Artifact generation.}
The branch includes a single render entrypoint,
\texttt{tools/render\_spt\_reg\_paper\_artifacts.py}, which writes the
synthetic, small-tabular, and larger YearPredictionMSD-style summary JSON
files by default, plus an artifact manifest used by the paper reproducibility
notes. The default Year slot uses a \textbf{synthetic} local CSV unless
\texttt{--year-cache-path} (or OpenML fetch flags) points at real OpenML Year;
manuscript numbers for the large-tabular track must come from a
\textbf{real-cache} run (see \texttt{reproducibility.md} and local bundle output
under \texttt{docs/research/sage\_reg\_results/.../tabular\_paper\_bundle/spt/full/}).
Paper-scale SPT-only refresh (full + audit profiles, optional extras, Stage-A sweep hook)
is \texttt{scripts/run\_neurips\_spt\_reg\_full.py}. A legacy combined SAGE+SPT
bundle remains \texttt{scripts/run\_tabular\_paper\_bundle.sh}. A longer-run
competitiveness plan (extra DA baselines, datasets) is in
\texttt{docs/research/paper\_strong\_experiment\_suite.md}.
An optional flag adds the local photo-$z$ compatibility benchmark. This
keeps the manuscript tied to committed outputs rather than ad hoc notebook
tables while avoiding a hard dependency on the older photo-$z$ surface in this
repository.

\section{Limitations}

SPT-Reg is expected to degrade when the target shift is primarily
non-transportable, such as severe conditional mechanism drift outside the
learned predictive subspace. The paper should report these failure modes
explicitly and separate validity claims from efficiency claims.

The discretized prior-transport stage is also an approximation to continuous
output shift, not a claim that regression label shift is solved in full
generality. The paper therefore needs to show when transport helps, when it
merely supports valid conformal or PPI correction, and when small-label target
refitting remains the stronger option.

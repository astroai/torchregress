\begin{abstract}
Semi-supervised regression often relies on scalar confidence scores to weight
unlabeled data, yet such scores can be poorly aligned with epistemic error in
the full predictive law. We present \emph{SAGE-Reg}, a prototype framework in
which unlabeled samples are trusted when \emph{distributional} predictions are
stable under stochastic input views. Multiple predictive laws are mapped into a
shared representation, a consensus law is formed, cross-view disagreement is
converted into trust weights, and training combines supervised loss with a
weighted distributional agreement term. A reference implementation supports
Gaussian, quantile, and histogram heads through a common
\texttt{PredictiveBatch} container in \texttt{torchregress}. We summarize
synthetic stress tests and real tabular benchmarks; the in-repo claim boundary
emphasizes safety relative to confidence-weighted pseudo-labeling until
multi-seed evidence supports stronger comparisons to supervised-only training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Uncertainty-guided pseudo-labeling and confidence weighting are standard in
semi-supervised learning~\citep{chapelle2006semisupervised}, but in regression they often collapse to a single
variance-like score that may not reflect when the entire predictive law is
unreliable. SAGE-Reg instead asks whether \emph{full} predictive distributions
agree when the input is perturbed stochastically---a signal that is sensitive
to epistemic instability rather than only to a scalar width proxy.

Consistency regularization and teacher EMAs are common in deep
semi-supervision~\citep{tarvainen2017mean}; ensemble-based uncertainty estimates
provide complementary signals for when predictions disagree~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017deep}.
The implementation target in \texttt{torchregress} is intentionally narrow:
one-dimensional targets, a shared support-grid density interface for
non-Gaussian heads, and detached trust weights so the model cannot trivially
inflate agreement by manipulating its own weight channel. The empirical
narrative should separate (i) \emph{safety} relative to harmful scalar-weighted
pseudo-labeling from (ii) \emph{superiority} to fully supervised training on
clean real regression benchmarks; the latter requires stable multi-seed
evidence (see \texttt{status.md} on the branch).

\section{Method}

Let $\{\tilde{x}_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ denote stochastic views of an unlabeled input
$x$, and let $\{p_{\theta}^{(k)}(y \mid \tilde{x}_k)\}_{k=1}^{K}$ be the
corresponding predictive laws. Each view is embedded into a common predictive
representation (e.g.\ densities on a shared 1D grid). Define the consensus law
as the arithmetic mean density
\[
q_x(y) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} p_{\theta}^{(k)}(y \mid \tilde{x}_k).
\]
Define self-disagreement as the average pairwise divergence between views,
\[
A(x) =
\frac{2}{K(K-1)}
\sum_{k < \ell}
D\!\left(p_{\theta}^{(k)}(\cdot \mid \tilde{x}_k), p_{\theta}^{(\ell)}(\cdot \mid \tilde{x}_\ell)\right),
\]
where $D$ is a divergence chosen to match the representation (a Jensen--Shannon
style score on normalized grid densities in the prototype). Trust weights use a
temperature $\tau > 0$,
\[
w(x) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{A(x)}{\tau}\right),
\]
with optional \emph{batch-relative} refinements (z-scored disagreement within a
batch and top-$k$ trust gating) implemented in
\texttt{disagreement\_to\_weight}. The training objective combines labeled
supervision with a weighted distributional agreement term on unlabeled points,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SAGE-Reg}}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\text{sup}}
+ \lambda_{\text{u}}
\frac{\sum_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \operatorname{sg}(w(x))
\, S\!\left(p_{\theta}(\cdot \mid x), q_x\right)}
{\sum_{x \in \mathcal{U}} \operatorname{sg}(w(x))},
\]
where $\operatorname{sg}$ denotes stop-gradient on the weight path and
$S$ is a family-specific distributional score (e.g.\ Gaussian cross-entropy,
grid cross-entropy for quantile and bar heads).

The public API is centered on \texttt{SAGERegLoss}, \texttt{SAGERegAgreement},
and \texttt{SelfAgreementTrainer} in \texttt{torchregress.semi\_supervised}.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Semi-supervised learning (SSL).}
Modern deep SSL is dominated by consistency regularization, graph-based
propagation, and generative / hybrid objectives~\citep{chapelle2006semisupervised}.
Recent benchmarks emphasize that headline improvements can be fragile under
realistic protocol choices~\citep{oliver2018realistic}. Much of the classical
literature targets \emph{classification}; regression receives less uniform
benchmarking, but the same families of ideas apply: unlabeled regularizers that
encourage stable predictions under noise or adversarial perturbations
\citep{miyato2018virtual}, and latent-variable architectures that couple
supervised and unsupervised paths~\citep{rasmus2015semisupervised}.

\paragraph{Deep semi-supervised regression (NeurIPS-era lines).}
The last few years have seen a sharp uptick in \emph{regression-native} SSL that
goes beyond ``apply FixMatch to discretized bins.'' Representative threads
include contrastive objectives with ordinal structure recovered via spectral
ideas~\citep{dai2023semisupervised}, auxiliary ranking models that align
unlabeled structure with continuous targets~\citep{huang2024rankup}, and
heteroscedastic pseudo-label reweighting with bilevel
optimization~\citep{sun2025heteroscedastic}. These methods are state of the art
for exploiting unlabeled \emph{continuous} supervision signals; SAGE-Reg is
orthogonal in objective design (multi-view \emph{distributional} agreement with
detached trust) rather than a direct competitor on the same loss template.

\paragraph{Consistency training and teacher--student methods.}
Mean Teacher and related EMA teacher schemes stabilize SSL by matching student
predictions to a slowly moving teacher~\citep{tarvainen2017mean}. In regression,
this is typically instantiated as consistency on \emph{point} predictions (e.g.\
MSE between means) or on low-dimensional summaries---including the strong
NeurIPS-era regression baselines above when they employ teacher--student
mechanisms. Our \texttt{MeanTeacher} row is a deliberately simple Gaussian-MLP
instantiation to isolate the gap between \emph{mean} consistency and law-level
agreement. SAGE-Reg differs along two axes: (i) agreement is measured between
\emph{full} predictive laws under stochastic input views, not only scalar
outputs, and (ii) trust is a stop-gradient function of cross-view
\emph{distributional} disagreement, not a single homoscedastic variance proxy.

\paragraph{Uncertainty-aware training and pseudo-label weighting.}
Heteroscedastic and Bayesian deep learning distinguish aleatoric and epistemic
components for weighting or abstention~\citep{kendall2017multi}. Very recent SSR
work sharpens this axis with heteroscedastic pseudo-label
dynamics~\citep{sun2025heteroscedastic}. Scalar confidence channels remain the
workhorse for weighted pseudo-labeling in classical
SSL~\citep{chapelle2006semisupervised}. These signals can be miscalibrated
relative to whole-law error, which motivates distributional disagreement as an
alternative gating signal. Deep ensembles remain a strong generic baseline for
probabilistic regression and epistemic diversity~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017deep};
our backbone comparison track keeps Gaussian, quantile, and histogram heads
explicitly separated rather than collapsing them to a single scalar score.

\section{Experiments}

The experimental program on the branch is organized into:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item synthetic confidence-trap and self-agreement stress tests,
  \item backbone comparisons (Gaussian, quantile, bar / binned PDF),
  \item real tabular tracks: OpenML YearPredictionMSD-style regression and an
  optional FAIR Universe Higgs public OOD-style stress benchmark, and
  \item supervised-gap confirmation and multi-seed aggregation over fixed
  configurations selected from tuning sweeps.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Competing methods and baselines.}
Primary baselines include supervised-only training on labeled data,
Mean Teacher--style consistency training (\texttt{MeanTeacher}),
confidence-weighted pseudo-labeling (scalar weighting), and ablations of SAGE-Reg
(single view, no weighting, no EMA teacher) as implemented in the benchmark
scripts.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Tables report point error (RMSE), negative log-likelihood where defined,
CRPS-style scores, interval coverage and width, and training-time diagnostics
such as mean trust weight and mean disagreement.

\paragraph{Artifacts.}
Machine-readable summaries, CSVs, and figures are written under dated
directories in \texttt{docs/research/sage\_reg\_results/}; see
\texttt{reproducibility.md} for the mapping from scripts to outputs.
Recent evidence (see \texttt{papers/neurips\_sage\_reg/status.md} and
\texttt{docs/research/paper\_strong\_experiment\_suite.md}) highlights three
points: (i) tuned-configuration multi-seed Year runs can show a small
\textbf{mean} NLL deficit versus supervised-only while still strongly beating
confidence weighting; (ii) a fixed-hyperparameter labeled-budget sweep on OpenML
Year shows SAGE improving NLL in a \textbf{label-scarce} regime and supervised
winning when the labeled set is large; (iii) large-scale Higgs-style stress
runs support a \textbf{shift-robustness} story for OOD NLL with a documented
binary-target modeling caveat. Paper-scale refresh (single command, dated outputs under
\texttt{docs/research/sage\_reg\_results/}) is
\texttt{scripts/run\_neurips\_sage\_reg\_full.py} (use \texttt{--quick} for CI-sized
budgets). A legacy joint SAGE+SPT shell bundle remains
\texttt{scripts/run\_tabular\_paper\_bundle.sh}.

\section{Limitations}

The prototype restricts attention to univariate regression and uses grid-based
approximations for non-Gaussian heads. It does not include reconstruction
losses, curriculum pseudo-label relabeling, or RaC-centric scalar ranking as
the core objective. Claims about beating supervised-only on broad real tabular
regression should track \texttt{papers/neurips\_sage\_reg/status.md} and the
aggregated multi-seed JSON/CSV artifacts.
